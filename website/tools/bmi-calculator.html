What is Body Mass Index (BMI)?

Body Mass Index (BMI) is an anthropometric screening indicator developed by Adolphe Quetelet that correlates body weight with stature. While it does not quantify individual adipose distribution or distinguish muscular mass from skeletal density, it serves as the global clinical benchmark for evaluating epidemiological risks associated with underweight and obesity.

BMI = Weight (kg) / [Height (m)]²  |  Imperial: [Weight (lb) × 703] / [Height (in)]²

Understanding Your BMI Breakdown

Body Mass Index establishes four primary categories for adults aged 20 and over. Because stature is squared in the denominator, small changes in height produce noticeable shifts in the final score.

World Health Organization Adult Classification Tiers

  • Severe Thinness: Below 16.0 kg/m²
  • Moderate Thinness: 16.0 – 16.9 kg/m²
  • Mild Thinness: 17.0 – 18.4 kg/m²
  • Normal / Healthy Weight: 18.5 – 24.9 kg/m²
  • Overweight (Pre-obese): 25.0 – 29.9 kg/m²
  • Obesity Class I: 30.0 – 34.9 kg/m²
  • Obesity Class II: 35.0 – 39.9 kg/m²
  • Obesity Class III (Severe): 40.0 kg/m² and above

Clinical Limitations of BMI

BMI is a screening metric, not a comprehensive medical diagnosis. It does not measure body fat percentage, visceral fat around internal organs, or muscular density. Competitive bodybuilders and strength athletes frequently classify as "overweight" or "obese" despite extremely low body fat percentages. Always consult healthcare professionals and consider complementary metrics such as waist-to-hip ratio and lipid panels.
